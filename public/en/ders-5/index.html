Lesson 5

ILS approach and landing

  • Level: Advanced
  • Reading time: about 15 minutes
  • On autopilot and by hand

In Lesson 4 we took off from Istanbul; now we're arriving in Antalya. ILS (Instrument Landing System) is the precision approach system airlines use most. It guides the aircraft to the runway centreline and the correct descent angle using radio signals from the ground. In this lesson we'll fly an ILS approach and landing, first on autopilot and then by hand.

The explanation is simplified to make learning easier; always use the aircraft's checklists when you fly. Speeds and values are examples; real values depend on the aircraft's weight and the day's conditions.

1How does the ILS work?

The ILS is made up of two beams. The localizer (LOC) lines the aircraft up horizontally with the runway centreline. The glideslope (G/S) draws a descent path vertically, usually at 3 degrees. An aircraft following both comes down right onto the start of the runway.

Every approach has a minimum: the height below which you can't descend if you can't see the runway. On CAT I, the most common approach, it's about 200 feet. On CAT II and CAT III approaches the minimum is much lower, and the landing is done by the autopilot.

1
LOC captureThe aircraft captures the runway centreline and turns onto it, holding its altitude.
2
G/S captureThe aircraft comes in below the glideslope and starts descending when it reaches it.
3
1,000 ftAt this point the aircraft must be in landing configuration, at the right speed and on the right path. This is called a stabilised approach.
4
MinimumThe Fenix calls out “MINIMUM”. If you can see the runway you continue to land; if not, you go around.
5
Flare and RETARDVery close to the ground the nose is raised slightly (the flare), and on the “RETARD” callout the thrust levers are pulled to idle.

2Approach preparation

You prepare during cruise, before starting the descent. The first job is to listen to the destination's ATIS: the runway in use, wind, visibility and QNH all come from there.

F-PLN: Select the ILS approach for the runway given by the ATIS at the destination, plus the STAR. Make sure there are no discontinuities left in the route.

RAD NAV: The ILS frequency and runway course usually come up automatically; check them against the approach chart.

PERF APPR: Enter the destination QNH, temperature and wind. Enter the minimum: for CAT I, the decision altitude in BARO; for CAT II and III, the decision height in RADIO. Select the landing flap setting (CONF 3 or FULL). The aircraft calculates the final approach speed (VAPP) itself.

Finally, select the autobrake (usually LO or MED for a normal runway) and brief yourself on the approach: runway, minimum, go-around procedure.

3Descent

The top of descent (T/D) appears on the ND. When ATC clears you to descend, enter the cleared altitude in the FCU ALT window and push the knob; the aircraft goes into managed descent (DES) and sets the speed and profile itself. If the aircraft ends up above the profile, you can use the speed brakes.

As you approach the destination, the DECEL point appears on the ND. At this point the aircraft enters the approach phase and starts slowing down. If ATC slows you down early, you can also activate this phase manually from the PERF page.

4Configuration: Flaps and landing gear

As the aircraft slows down, you extend the flaps step by step. The PFD speed tape tells you when: once the speed drops below certain marks, you move to the next step.

After lowering the landing gear, ARM the ground spoilers. Every line on the ECAM landing memo (LDG MEMO) should be green: landing gear, signs, cabin, spoilers and flaps.

5Capturing the ILS

Press the LS button on the EFIS panel next to the FCU. Two deviation scales appear on the PFD: glideslope on the right, localizer at the bottom.

1
Glideslope (G/S)The magenta diamond shows where the glideslope is. If the diamond is centred, you're on the path. If the diamond is high, the path is above you, so you're low.
2
Localizer (LOC)If the diamond is centred, you're on the centreline. If the diamond is to the right, the centreline is to your right, so you're to the left.

Simple rule: fly towards the diamond. Wherever the diamond is, steer the aircraft that way.

When ATC clears you for the ILS approach, press the APPR button on the FCU. If you're going to autoland, engage the second autopilot (AP2) too. Then watch the FMA:

1
Modes armedG/S and LOC in cyan: armed, will engage when their turn comes. The fourth column shows approach capability; with two autopilots, CAT 3 DUAL, meaning autoland capable.
2
CapturedYou'll first see the capture phase with asterisks (LOC*, G/S*), then green LOC and G/S. The aircraft is now following both beams.
3
AutolandBelow 400 feet the two modes merge into LAND. Close to the ground you'll see FLARE, and ROLL OUT once the wheels touch down.

6Stabilised approach

When you reach 1,000 feet, ask yourself: Is the aircraft in landing configuration? Is the speed around VAPP? Am I on the LOC and G/S? Is my vertical speed below 1,000 feet per minute? Is the landing checklist complete?

If the answer to any of these is "no", you go around. That's not a failure; it's part of flying safely.

Cenk's tipDon't try to save a bad approach with a good landing. In real aviation, a significant share of accidents come from continuing to land from unstabilised approaches. Make going around a habit in the sim too; it's both safe and very instructive.

7Autoland

With both autopilots engaged and the FMA showing CAT 3 DUAL, the aircraft can land itself. Your job is to monitor: watch LAND, FLARE and ROLL OUT come up in turn. At about 10 feet the Fenix calls out “RETARD”; pull the thrust levers to IDLE straight away. The aircraft won't do this for you.

Once the wheels touch down, the aircraft tracks the runway centreline itself (ROLL OUT). Select reverse thrust and check the autobrake is working. As the speed drops to taxi speed, disconnect the autobrake by pressing the brakes, and disengage the autopilots.

8Manual landing

When you're ready to hand-fly, disconnect the autopilot early, say around 1,000 feet, with the red button on the sidestick. Keep the flight director (FD) and autothrust on; following the FD bars keeps you on the ILS, and the autothrust holds the speed.

At about 30 feet, raise the nose gently by a few degrees; this is called the flare. At about 20 feet, when you hear the “RETARD” callout, pull the thrust levers to IDLE. When the main wheels touch down, lower the nose gently, select reverse thrust and brake to slow down.

Cenk's tipDuring the flare, look at the far end of the runway, not just below the nose. Seeing the horizon gives you a much better feel for your height above the ground. Don't over-raise the nose; the Airbus likes a small, gentle flare.

9Go-around

If you can't see the runway at minimums, if the approach isn't stabilised, or if there's an obstruction on the runway, you go around. Push the thrust levers to TOGA; the FMA shows MAN TOGA, SRS and GA TRK. Retract the flaps one stage and raise the gear with a positive climb. The aircraft follows the missed approach procedure in the MCDU. Then tell ATC and get ready for another approach.

✓Landing checklist

On final approach
  • ILS frequency and runway course correct, LS on
  • QNH, wind and minimum entered on PERF APPR
  • Landing gear down, flaps at landing setting
  • Spoilers ARM, autobrake selected
  • Every line on the LDG MEMO green
  • Stabilised at 1,000 ft

What's the difference between the localizer and the glideslope?

The localizer guides the aircraft horizontally to the runway centreline; the glideslope guides it vertically along a descent path of about 3 degrees.

The G/S diamond on the PFD is above centre. Where is the aircraft relative to the glideslope?

Below it. The diamond shows where the path is; if the diamond is high, the path is above you.

The FMA shows LOC and G/S in cyan. What does that mean?

The modes are armed but not yet active. When the aircraft captures the beams, they first show asterisks, then turn green.

At 1,000 feet your speed is 20 knots above VAPP. What should you do?

The approach isn't stabilised; you should go around.

During an autoland you hear the “RETARD” callout. What should you do?

Pull the thrust levers to IDLE straight away. Even in an autoland, the pilot does this.

Congratulations, you've landed in Antalya! In the final lesson we'll fly the same flight on IVAO or VATSIM with real controllers. ✈️
