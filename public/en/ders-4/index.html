Lesson 4

Takeoff from a cold & dark cockpit

  • Level: Intermediate
  • Reading time: about 15 minutes
  • Fenix A319 · A320 · A321

"Cold & dark" is when the aircraft is completely shut down: the screens are dark, the engines are cold, not a single light is on. In this lesson we'll take the Fenix from that state and get it airborne with the LTFM–LTAI plan we prepared in Lesson 3, following the order a real crew would.

What's described here is a simplified flow to make learning easier. Real procedures are much more detailed; always use the aircraft's checklists when you fly. Sample speeds and values are based on the LTFM–LTAI plan.

1The overall flow

The order isn't random. First we power up the aircraft, then we start the long-running tasks (like ADIRS alignment) early so they finish in the background while we do the rest of the preparation.

2Power: Waking the aircraft up

Press the BAT 1 and BAT 2 buttons on the overhead panel. Before switching the batteries on, check their voltage; they should be above 25.5 volts. Then connect the ground power unit (GPU) from the Fenix's tablet (EFB); when the green AVAIL appears on the EXT PWR button on the overhead, press it. The screens now come to life.

Cenk's tipLearn Airbus's "dark cockpit" philosophy: a button with no light on the overhead means the system is working normally and automatically. If you see a light, something is either off or needs attention. When you've finished preparing, your goal is an overhead with almost no lights left on.

3APU and air

The APU is a small jet engine in the tail; on the ground it supplies electricity and the air needed to start the engines. Press the APU MASTER SW button, wait a few seconds, then press APU START. The APU page opens on the ECAM. When the green AVAIL appears on the button, the APU is ready; switch on APU BLEED to supply air to the cabin and the air conditioning.

4Overhead scan

Real pilots scan the overhead panel in a set pattern, left to right and top to bottom. The numbers in the diagram show the sections covered in this lesson.

5Preparing the MCDU: DIFRIPS

Pilots use an acronym so they don't forget any MCDU pages: DIFRIPS. Each letter stands for a page to fill in, in order.

On the INIT A page, pull in the SimBrief plan with INIT REQUEST (Lesson 3). On the F-PLN page, select the departure runway and SID; they must match the runway and procedure given by ATC. On INIT B, compare the zero fuel weight (ZFW) and block fuel with the OFP.

The PERF page is the heart of the takeoff. Take the takeoff speeds (V1, VR, V2), the flexible takeoff temperature (FLEX) and the flap setting from the performance calculator on the Fenix tablet and enter them here. In our example: V1 141, VR 142, V2 146, FLEX 58, flaps 1+F.

6FCU and final preparation

On the FCU, enter the initial altitude given by ATC (for example 6000 ft) in the ALT window. Turn on the flight directors (FD) on both sides, set the altimeter to the day's QNH and set the ND to a suitable range for takeoff. Enter the squawk code given by ATC in the transponder.

7Pushback and engine start

Check that the doors are closed and switch on the BEACON light; it tells everyone on the ground "engines are about to start". Start the pushback from the Fenix's tablet.

Engine start on the Airbus is largely automatic. Turn the engine mode selector (ENG MODE) to IGN/START and switch on ENG 2 MASTER first. On the ECAM, watch N2 rise, fuel flow start and EGT increase, in that order. Once the engine settles at idle, do the same for ENG 1.

With both engines running: set ENG MODE to NORM, switch off APU BLEED, ARM the ground spoilers, set the flaps for takeoff (1+F) and set the trim to the value calculated by the tablet. Then do the flight control check: move the sidestick and pedals to full deflection in every direction and watch the surfaces move on the ECAM F/CTL page.

8Taxi and holding point

Set the nose light to TAXI, set the autobrake to MAX (it gives the strongest braking on a rejected takeoff) and start taxiing. While taxiing, press the T.O CONFIG button. Check that every line in the ECAM takeoff memo (T.O MEMO) is green: autobrake, signs, cabin, spoilers, flaps and takeoff config.

Lining up on the runway: STROBE and landing lights on, nose light T.O, TCAS TA/RA.

9Takeoff

On the Airbus, the thrust levers have fixed positions (detents). FLX/MCT is used for most takeoffs: the aircraft doesn't use more thrust than it needs, based on the FLEX temperature you entered on the PERF page, which saves the engines.

First bring the thrust levers to about 50% N1 and wait for both engines to stabilise. Then push the levers to the FLX/MCT detent and check the FMA straight away:

1
Takeoff rollMAN FLX +58: thrust manually at the FLEX setting. SRS: the mode that controls speed during takeoff and climb. RWY: runway centreline guidance. Cyan NAV and A/THR: armed, will engage when their turn comes.
2
Thrust reduction altitudeAt a set altitude (usually 1,500 ft), LVR CLB flashes in a box. Pull the thrust levers back to the CL detent; the autothrust takes over and you usually leave the levers there for the rest of the flight.

Sequence: At 100 knots, cross-check the speed indications. Keep your hand on the thrust levers until V1, so you can reject the takeoff if something goes wrong. At VR, gently pull the sidestick back and raise the nose to about 15 degrees; follow the FD bars. With a positive climb, retract the landing gear. Above 100 feet you can engage the autopilot (AP1). At thrust reduction, bring the levers back to CL. As the aircraft accelerates, F and S marks appear on the PFD speed tape; retract the flaps step by step as the speed passes them, and finally disarm the spoilers.

Cenk's tipThe most critical moment of the takeoff is right after you push the levers to FLX/MCT. Take a second to read the FMA: if you see MAN FLX, SRS, RWY, the aircraft is doing what you want. If you don't, the safest moment to stop and think is while you're still slow.

✓Final pre-takeoff check

On the runway, just before takeoff
  • ADIRS aligned, PFD and ND normal
  • PERF speeds and FLEX entered in the MCDU
  • Flaps 1+F, trim set, spoilers ARM
  • Every line on the T.O MEMO green
  • Strobe and landing lights on, TCAS TA/RA
  • Takeoff clearance received

?Test yourself

Click a question to see the answer.

Why do we switch the ADIRS on at the start of the preparation?

Alignment takes a few minutes and the aircraft mustn't move during that time. Starting it early means it finishes in the background while we do the rest of the preparation.

A button on the overhead panel has no light at all. What does that mean?

Under the dark cockpit philosophy, it means the system is working normally and automatically.

Which page is the P in DIFRIPS, and what do you enter there?

The PERF page. You enter the takeoff speeds (V1, VR, V2), the FLEX temperature and the flap/trim setting.

You've pushed the thrust levers to FLX/MCT. What should you see in the first column of the FMA?

MAN FLX and the FLEX temperature (+58 in our example).

A boxed, flashing LVR CLB has appeared on the FMA. What should you do?

Pull the thrust levers back to the CL detent. The autothrust then takes over and you switch to climb thrust.
