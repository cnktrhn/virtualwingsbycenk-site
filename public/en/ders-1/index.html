Lesson 1

Installing and setting up MSFS

  • Level: Beginner
  • Reading time: about 6 minutes
  • Microsoft Flight Simulator

Every good flight starts with a properly prepared simulator. In this lesson we'll install Microsoft Flight Simulator and get it ready for your first flight. Take your time: the settings you make here will make every lesson that follows easier.

1Before you install

MSFS is a big program, and it only gets bigger as you add scenery and aircraft. If you can, install it on a fast SSD and leave plenty of free space for add-ons. Update your graphics card driver; many visual and performance problems come from outdated drivers.

2Graphics settings

Our goal isn't the prettiest picture, it's the smoothest flight. Start with the "Medium" preset, then raise it to suit your system. The settings with the biggest impact on visuals and performance are terrain level of detail (Terrain LOD), cloud quality and render scaling. If you have an NVIDIA RTX card, try DLSS; on most systems it gives a serious performance boost.

Cenk's tipBeing able to read the cockpit displays clearly matters more than the detail on the trees outside. Prioritise your settings accordingly.

3Control settings

Set up your joystick, throttle and pedals one by one in the Settings → Controls menu. Create a clean profile for each device and remove conflicting default bindings. Tune your axis sensitivity: a small deadzone on the joystick stops the aircraft wobbling when your hand trembles.

If you fly the Fenix, calibrate the throttle detents (CL, FLX/MCT, TOGA) in the Fenix's own settings. I'll show this separately in a later lesson.

4Assistance options

If you want to fly realistically, set the assistance options to the "True to life" preset. Turn off helpers like auto-rudder, simplified fuel management and visual route lines. It may feel hard at first, but it's the only way to learn real procedures.

5General settings

Set the units to aviation standards: feet for altitude, knots for speed and hPa for pressure. Turn on traffic to bring the airports to life. If you're going to fly online (IVAO, VATSIM), turn off AI traffic so it doesn't mix with real pilots.

✓Checklist

Before you fly
  • Graphics driver up to date
  • Smooth graphics (aim for at least 30 FPS)
  • All axes in the right direction with the right sensitivity
  • Assistance set to "True to life"
  • Units set to feet, knots and hPa

If everything checks out, you're ready. In the next lesson we'll get to know the Airbus cockpit together. ✈️
