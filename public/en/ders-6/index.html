Lesson 6

Flying online: VATSIM and IVAO

  • Level: Advanced
  • Reading time: about 15 minutes
  • Radio with real controllers

Up to now you've flown on your own. In this lesson we'll share the sky: VATSIM and IVAO are free networks where thousands of virtual pilots and volunteer air traffic controllers from all over the world fly at the same time. The controllers use real procedures and real radio phraseology. This is where simulation gets most exciting.

In the sample exchanges, the departure procedure, taxi route and frequencies are shown in square brackets; when you fly, take the real values from the charts and ATC.

1VATSIM or IVAO?

Both are free, both are very good, and both have an active Turkish community. I fly on both. The most important question when choosing: which one has more controllers online when you fly, and which one do your friends fly on?

2Before you start

Create an account: Sign up for free on your chosen network's website. After registering you may be asked to complete a short introduction or training step; do it, it's genuinely useful.

Install the pilot client: Altitude for IVAO, vPilot for VATSIM. The client connects to MSFS, sends your aircraft to the network, brings other aircraft into your sim and carries the radio audio. Set up a microphone and a push-to-talk key; using a button on your joystick is very handy.

Prepare your sim: Turn off AI traffic in MSFS (Lesson 1). Other aircraft will now be real pilots.

3Filing your flight plan

When you fly online, ATC sees your plan, so you need to file it before connecting. The easiest way is to send the SimBrief plan you made in Lesson 3 straight to the network using SimBrief's prefile option.

In the plan remarks you state how you'll communicate: /V/ voice, /R/ listen by voice and reply by text, /T/ text only. If you're not ready to talk yet, starting with /T/ is completely normal.

Cenk's tipOn your first flights, write "NEW MEMBER" in the plan remarks. Controllers will see it and speak more slowly and helpfully. There's no shame in being new; we all pressed that transmit button for the first time once.

4Who will you talk to?

On one flight you talk to several controllers. Each is responsible for one phase of the flight and hands you over to the next.

On online networks, not every position is always staffed. The rule is simple: top-down coverage. For example, if Delivery is closed in Istanbul, you ask Ground for your clearance; if Ground is closed too, you ask Tower. If none of them is online and only Ankara Control is, you ask them for everything.

If there are no controllers at all, switch to the UNICOM frequency (122.800) and announce what you're doing with short text messages, so nearby pilots know where you are.

5Radio phraseology basics

The language of aviation radio is English, and it has a short, clear pattern. On your first call you say four things: who you're calling (Istanbul Tower), who you are (VWC001), where you are (holding point runway 35L) and what you want (ready for departure).

Readback: Always read back the clearances, altitudes, headings, speeds, runway, squawk code and frequencies the controller gives you, and add your callsign at the end. That way the controller confirms you've understood correctly.

Letters and numbers: To avoid confusion, letters are spoken as code words. Numbers have three special pronunciations too: 3 "tree", 5 "fife", 9 "niner".

6Sample flight: LTFM to LTAI

This time we're flying online the flight we've been planning since Lesson 3. Below are the exchanges for the four most important moments. The gold bubbles are your words; the blue bubbles are the controller's.

Getting your clearance · Istanbul Delivery

Istanbul Delivery, VWC001, stand C12, information Alpha, request IFR clearance to Antalya.

VWC001, cleared to Antalya via [SID] departure, runway 35L, climb initially 6000 feet, squawk 4521.

Cleared to Antalya, [SID] departure, runway 35L, climb 6000 feet, squawk 4521, VWC001.

VWC001, readback correct, contact Istanbul Ground [frequency].

Takeoff · Istanbul Tower

Istanbul Tower, VWC001, holding point runway 35L, ready for departure.

VWC001, wind 340 degrees 8 knots, runway 35L, cleared for takeoff.

Runway 35L, cleared for takeoff, VWC001.

Approach · Antalya Approach

Antalya Approach, VWC001, descending flight level 80, information Bravo.

VWC001, descend 3000 feet, cleared ILS approach runway 36C, report established.

Descend 3000 feet, cleared ILS approach runway 36C, wilco, VWC001.

Landing · Antalya Tower

Antalya Tower, VWC001, established ILS runway 36C.

VWC001, wind 010 degrees 6 knots, runway 36C, cleared to land.

Runway 36C, cleared to land, VWC001.

When a controller hands you over to the next ("contact Ankara Control [frequency]"), read back the frequency too, switch to the new frequency and check in with your altitude.

Cenk's tipBefore your first online flight, just listen for a few days. Opening a busy airport on a live air traffic listening site or on the network's live map and listening to the controllers gets the patterns into your ear very quickly.

7Online etiquette

A good online pilot
  • Connects at the gate (on stand), never on the runway or in the air
  • Listens to the frequency for a few seconds before talking
  • Thinks about what to say, then speaks briefly and clearly
  • Says "say again" without hesitation when they don't understand
  • Sets the squawk code and frequencies correctly
  • Tells ATC if they have to leave before the flight is over

?Test yourself

Click a question to see the answer.

In Istanbul, Delivery and Ground are closed and Tower is open. Who do you ask for your clearance?

Tower. Under the top-down coverage rule, if the lower positions are closed, the one above takes over their job.

What do you do if there are no controllers at all?

Switch to the UNICOM frequency (122.800) and announce what you're doing with short text messages.

What does /T/ mean in the plan remarks?

That you'll communicate by text only. /V/ means voice, /R/ means listening by voice and replying by text.

The controller says "climb flight level 240". What should your reply be?

"Climb flight level 240, VWC001." You always read back altitude instructions and add your callsign at the end.

Where should you connect to the network?

At the gate, on stand. Connecting on the runway or in the air endangers other traffic.
