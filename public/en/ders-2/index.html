Lesson 2

Getting to know the Airbus cockpit

  • Level: Beginner
  • Reading time: about 12 minutes
  • A319 · A320 · A321

At first glance the Airbus cockpit can be intimidating: six screens, hundreds of buttons and constantly changing coloured text. The good news is that there's logic to all of it. In this lesson we'll learn to read four key parts: the PFD, ND, ECAM and FCU. Once you understand them, eighty percent of the cockpit will fall into place.

The diagrams are simplified drawings for teaching; the real displays have more detail. Everything here is the same in the Fenix A319, A320 and A321.

1Cockpit overview

On the main panel, each pilot has two screens: the PFD on the outside (the screen you fly from) and the ND on the inside (the map showing where you're going). In the centre, two ECAM screens, upper and lower, monitor the aircraft's systems. The long panel just above the screens is the FCU; this is where you tell the autopilot what to do. On the centre console below are the MCDUs, where you enter the flight plan.

2The language of colours

Colours on Airbus displays aren't random. Learn this table by heart and you'll know at a glance what's normal and what needs your attention.

GreenActive modes and normal values
CyanPilot-selected values, armed modes and actions to carry out
MagentaTargets and constraints managed by the computer (FMGC)
AmberCaution: a situation to keep an eye on
RedWarning: immediate action required
YellowAircraft symbol and reference lines
WhiteTitles, labels and scales

3PFD: Primary flight display

This is where your eyes spend most of the flight. The aircraft's attitude is in the middle, speed on the left, altitude and vertical speed on the right, and heading at the bottom. The strip across the top shows what the automation is doing at that moment.

1
FMA (flight mode annunciator)Made up of five columns: autothrust mode, vertical mode, lateral mode, approach capability and AP/FD/A/THR status. Green text shows the active mode; cyan text shows an armed mode that will engage when its turn comes. In the example the aircraft is climbing (CLB), following the route (NAV) and will level off at the target altitude (cyan ALT).
2
Speed tapeThe yellow line shows the current speed. The yellow arrow shows the speed you'll reach in 10 seconds (the trend). The magenta triangle is the computer's target speed; if you select the speed yourself it turns cyan. The amber band at the bottom warns you're approaching the minimum safe speed (VLS), and the red-and-black band at the top the maximum speed.
3
Attitude indicatorBlue sky, brown ground. The yellow symbols are the aircraft itself. When hand-flying, the green flight director (FD) bars tell you where to steer: keep the yellow symbol where the bars cross.
4
Altitude tapeThe yellow box shows the current altitude. The cyan value at the top is the target altitude you selected on the FCU. STD at the very bottom means standard pressure (1013 hPa) is set; down low you'll see the QNH value instead.
5
Vertical speedThe green needle shows how many feet per minute you're climbing or descending. The numbers are in hundreds: 15 means 1,500 feet per minute.
6
HeadingThe yellow line is the direction the aircraft's nose is pointing. The cyan triangle is the heading you selected on the FCU.
Cenk's tipReal Airbus pilots call out every change on the FMA: "THR CLB, CLB, NAV, ALT blue, AP1". It may sound odd, but it makes you see what the aircraft is actually doing instead of assuming. Build this habit in the sim too.

4ND: Navigation display

The ND is your map. It shows the flight plan, the upcoming waypoints, the wind and, if you like, weather radar or terrain. You change its view and range from the EFIS control panel above the screens.

1
Ground speed, true airspeed and windGS is your speed over the ground, TAS your speed through the air. Below that is the wind direction and speed (from 275° at 42 knots); the green arrow shows where the wind is coming from relative to the aircraft.
2
Next waypoint (TO waypoint)The name, bearing, distance and estimated arrival time of the waypoint the aircraft is heading for.
3
Heading markThe yellow line on the compass shows where the aircraft's nose is pointing.
4
Flight planThe green line is the active route. The white waypoint is the next one; the green waypoints follow.
5
Aircraft symbolThe aircraft's position on the map.
6
Range ringShows half of the selected range. 80 NM is selected here, so the ring marks 40 NM.

ND modes: ROSE shows the full compass (with ILS, VOR and NAV options); it's used a lot on approach. ARC shows the 90 degrees ahead of you in large scale; ideal for cruise. PLAN shows the map north-up; used to check the flight plan. Range can be set from 10 to 320 NM.

5ECAM: The aircraft's health screen

ECAM is made up of two screens. The upper E/WD shows the engines and warnings; the lower SD shows system details. When something fails, ECAM tells you what the problem is and lists what to do step by step.

1
Engine indicationsN1 (fan speed, the main thrust indication), EGT (exhaust gas temperature), N2 (core speed) and FF (fuel flow). Total fuel on board (FOB) and slat/flap position are at the top right.
2
Message areaGreen lines are memos. Amber is a caution, red a warning. The cyan lines underneath are actions for you to carry out: each disappears from the list as you complete it.
3
System pageChanges automatically with the flight phase: doors on the ground, engines on takeoff, the CRUISE page in cruise, and so on. You can also call up pages like ENG, BLEED, ELEC, HYD and FUEL manually from the ECAM control panel on the centre console.
4
Permanent dataOutside air temperatures (TAT, SAT), the time and the aircraft's gross weight (GW). Always here, whichever page is open.

6FCU: Talking to the autopilot

The FCU is the panel where you tell the autopilot and autothrust what you want them to do. It has four windows and four knobs: speed, heading, altitude and vertical speed.

1
Speed / MachThe target speed. The SPD/MACH button next to it switches between knots and Mach.
2
Heading / TrackThe target heading. The HDG V/S button switches heading and vertical speed to track and flight path angle (TRK FPA). The LOC button is only used to capture the ILS localizer.
3
Autopilot buttonsAP1 and AP2 engage and disengage the autopilots, A/THR the autothrust. APPR arms the approach mode; EXPED speeds up a climb or descent.
4
AltitudeThe target altitude. The aircraft will never go past it; it's the limit of any climb or descent.
5
Vertical speedYou select how many feet per minute you want to climb or descend.

The most important rule: push or pull. The FCU knobs turn, and they can also be pushed and pulled. Pull a knob and the aircraft follows the value you've chosen; this is called selected mode, and the value shows in cyan on the PFD. Push it and you hand control to the computer; this is called managed mode. The aircraft follows the flight plan and speed profile in the MCDU; the window shows dashes and a dot (---•), and the targets on the PFD turn magenta.

Cenk's tipWhen you're confused, look at the FMA on the PFD, not the FCU. Even if you can't remember whether you pushed or pulled, the FMA tells you the truth: green HDG means you're on a selected heading, green NAV means you're following the flight plan.

✓Test yourself

Click a question to see the answer.

The target speed on the PFD is magenta. Who's controlling the speed?

The computer (FMGC). Magenta means a managed target. If you had selected the speed yourself, it would be cyan.

The vertical mode column of the FMA shows a cyan "ALT". What does that mean?

ALT mode is armed but not yet active. When the aircraft reaches the target altitude set on the FCU, it will level off and ALT will turn green.

You've set the ND range to 80 NM. How many NM does the inner dashed ring show?

40 NM. The ring always shows half the selected range.

There are cyan lines under an amber message on the ECAM. What should you do?

The cyan lines are actions to carry out. Work through them in order; each line drops off the list as you complete it.

You pulled the HDG knob on the FCU. What does the aircraft do?

It switches to selected mode and turns to the heading in the window. The lateral mode on the FMA changes from NAV to HDG. To go back to the flight plan, you push the knob.

If you got them all, you've mastered the cockpit. In the next lesson we'll put together our first flight plan in SimBrief. ✈️
